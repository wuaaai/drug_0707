ABSTRACT
With the deepening integration of artificial intelligence in healthcare, Combinatorial Medication Recommendation (CMR) demonstrates immense potential value as a critical task for clinical decision support. However, existing methods relying on standardized codes (e.g., ICD, ATC) often neglect valuable uncoded data, particularly infusion records, leading to a failure in capturing the Therapeutic Synergy between infusion and prescription medications. Furthermore, current graph models often treat diverse clinical relationships as homogeneous structures, failing to synthesize distinct relational views or align topological structures with intrinsic semantic definitions. To address these limitations, we propose a synergy-aware recommendation framework named SynGSA-Rec. Our framework introduces the use of textual descriptions to explicitly model latent Therapeutic Synergy, overcoming the inherent limitations of code-dependent methods by unlocking infusion records. We mine textual descriptions of both infusion and prescription medications to explicitly encode the therapeutic logic bridging immediate intervention and stable maintenance. Additionally, we design a novel two-stage contrastive learning mechanism to recalibrate entity representations. Specifically, we construct multiple clinical subgraphs to decouple interactions into pairwise relational views. In the first stage, we fuse these views to generate unified graph embeddings. Crucially, in the second stage, we align these embeddings with textual semantics. Finally, we employ temporal aggregation to construct a comprehensive patient profile, serving as a robust basis for generating optimal medication combinations. Experiments on the MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV datasets demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines.

1. Introduction
The widespread adoption of Electronic Health Records (EHR) has generated massive amounts of clinical data, serving as the foundation for intelligent applications. Among these, Combinatorial Medication Recommendation (CMR), a critical clinical decision support task aiming to provide effective medication combinations for patients with complex health conditions, has received increasing attention in recent years (Ali et al., 2023; Mishra and Shridevi, 2024; Liu et al., 2024a).
Most existing deep learning models for CMR typically rely on data in EHR that have clear, standardized coding systems--such as diseases (ICD_CM codes), procedures (ICD_PCS codes), and prescription medications (ATC/NDC codes) (Wu et al., 2022). These methods often integrate such structured clinical records with external knowledge like medication chemical structures(Yang et al. 2021b; Yang et al. 2023) to improve recommendation performance. While effective, this heavy reliance on standardized codes leads to a critical limitation: valuable clinical data lacking such codes are often discarded. A prime example is infusion medications in ICU settings, which are frequently recorded as unstructured text or non-standardized item IDs in databases like MIMIC (Johnson et al., 2016, 2023), and are thus largely ignored by current models.
However, ignoring infusion medications means missing a crucial part of the clinical picture: Immediate Intervention.

In real-world practice, especially in the ICU, treatment is a dynamic, staged process transitioning from immediate intervention (often via infusion) to stable maintenance (Devlin et al., 2018)(via prescription). There exists a profound Therapeutic Synergy between these two stages, which collectively form a coherent therapeutic strategy.
Consider a patient with a malignant brain tumor accompanied by pain. The patient's infusion records include Acetaminophen-IV, used for rapid relief of moderate to severe pain. Meanwhile, the prescription records include oral Acetaminophen and the compound OxyCODONE-Acetaminophen, used for long-term pain management. This treatment regimen presents clear clinical logic: acute pain

This example illustrates the logical connection existing between infusion and prescription medications. Existing methods typically do not use infusion data due to the lack of standard codes, and thus often overlook this clinical logic chain. We argue that leveraging textual descriptions (Bai et al., 2025) to capture the latent synergy between infusion and prescription medications offers a promising avenue to enhance the clinical logicality of CMR. To address this limit, we propose SynGSA-Rec (SynergyAware Medication Recommendation with Graph-Semantic Alignment), a progressive framework designed to capture and leverage this therapeutic synergy. First, SynGSA-Rec explicitly models the synergistic patterns between infusion and prescription medications by mining their textual descriptions, generating synergy-aware medication representations. Building upon this foundation, we further introduce a novel two-stage contrastive learning mechanism (Li et al., 2023; An et al., 2023). Specifically, we construct multiple clinical subgraphs to decouple complex interactions into pairwise relational views (e.g., disease-medication). In the first stage, these views undergo contrasting to fuse into unified graph embeddings. Crucially, in the second stage, we apply graph-text contrasting specifically to align these graph embeddings with textual semantics. For medications, we uniquely enhance this alignment by dynamically enriching their textual semantic view with the therapeutic synergy patterns learned from our first module. This synergy-aware alignment ensures that the final medication representations are not only structurally comprehensive but also therapeutically coherent with the captured synergistic patterns. The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:
· We are the first to explicitly model the Therapeutic Synergy between infusion and prescription medications by leveraging textual descriptions, thereby incorporating a vital yet previously overlooked dimension of clinical logic in EHR.
· We propose a novel two-stage contrastive learning mechanism. It fuses complementary relational views via subgraph contrasting and uniquely aligns the resulting graph embeddings with their textual semantics.
· Extensive experiments on large-scale public medical datasets demonstrate that SynGSA-Rec achieves significant improvements over state-of-the-art baseline models across multiple key evaluation metrics, verifying the effectiveness of our synergy-guided approach.

2. Related work
In this section, we review related work in three key areas: code-driven medication recommendation, text-enhanced clinical representation, and cross-view alignment via contrastive learning.
2.1. Code-Driven Medication Recommendation The field of medication recommendation has evolved
from processing simple sequential patterns to modeling complex entity interactions, yet it remains fundamentally anchored in standardized clinical coding systems (e.g., ICD, ATC). Early approaches primarily focused on longitudinal patient history, utilizing Recurrent Neural Networks or Gated Recurrent Units to capture temporal dependencies in diseases and treatment sequences (Choi et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2017). To better capture the structural relationships between medical entities, subsequent studies introduced Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to model the co-occurrence of diseases and medications as heterogeneous graphs (Shang et al., 2019; Zheng et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021). Recently, to bridge the semantic gap in sparse data, state-ofthe-art methods have integrated external knowledge into these graph structures. For instance, (Yang et al., 2021b) and (Yang et al., 2023) incorporate molecular structures (2D graphs or substructures) to explicitly model drug-drug interactions (DDI), while methods like (Yang et al., 2021a) leverage dynamic changes in patient status. Despite these advancements, these methods share a critical limitation: a heavy reliance on structured, standardized codes. This codedependency causes them to overlook valuable unstructured clinical data, most notably infusion mdications in ICU settings, which often lack standard coding. Consequently, existing models fail to capture the crucial therapeutic synergy between infusion medications and prescription medications, leading to a break in the logical chain of clinical treatment (Zhang et al., 2025).
2.2. Text-Enhanced Clinical Representation Recognizing the information loss inherent in structured
codes, another line of research explores leveraging the rich unstructured text in Electronic Health Records (EHR). With the advent of Pre-trained Language Models (PLMs) such as BERT and its clinical variants (e.g., (Huang et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2020; Gu et al., 2021)), researchers have begun to utilize textual descriptions to enhance medical entity representations. Current approaches typically treat text as a supplementary feature source, encoding descriptions of diseases or medications into semantic vectors that are concatenated with code-based embeddings to enrich representation quality (Kim et al., 2025; Móstoles et al., 2024). Other works frame recommendation as a matching problem, measuring the semantic compatibility between a patient's textual clinical notes and medication documents. (Ling et al., 2014). However, in these frameworks, text is generally utilized as a static source of auxiliary information or for simple semantic matching. Existing methods neither leverage text to uncover clinical logic within underutilized data

2.3. Cross-View Alignment via Contrastive Learning
Contrastive Learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for aligning representations across different views or modalities, achieving remarkable success in fields like Computer Vision (Radford et al., 2021)and molecular representation learning (aligning 2D and 3D molecular graphs) (Stärk et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2022). In the medical domain, recent works have applied contrastive learning to align patient EHR views with medical knowledge graphs or to harmonize medical imaging with textual reports (Zhang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022; Wen et al., 2025). The core intuition is to maximize the mutual information between different views of the same entity to learn a more robust and distinct representation. Inspired by BiMoRec's (Mu et al., 2025) use of contrastive learning to fuse molecular modalities,similarly, the NLA-MMR framework (Tan et al., 2024) demonstrates the efficacy of using natural language to assist multi-modal alignment in medication recommendation, further validating the trend of integrating textual semantics. Building on these insights, we identify a unique opportunity in medication recommendation: the alignment of an entity's topological context within the clinical graph with its intrinsic textual semantics. However, standard cross view alignment is insufficient for complex clinical scenarios. Unlike previous works, our proposed SynGSA-Rec introduces a novel graphsemantic alignment mechanism that specifically targets the latent Therapeutic Synergy. By mining the textual descriptions of infusion and prescription medications to generate a synergy-aware semantic view, and then contrasting this view with the graph representation, we ensure that the learned representations are not only structurally context-aware but also clinically logically coherent.

5.4. Setup Protocol In this subsection, we offer a comprehensive overview
of the experimental environment setup, parameter selection, model configuration, and sampling methods for the testing phase
5.4.1. Experimental Environment The experiments were conducted on an Ubuntu 22.04
system equipped with 30GB of memory, 12 CPUs, and a 24GB NVIDIA RTX3090 GPU, using PyTorch 2.0.0 and CUDA 11.7.
5.4.2. Configuration and Parameter For the embedding matrices of entities (Diseases, Proce-
dures, and Medications), we set 128 as the embedding size, initialized using Xavier uniform initialization. The global dropout rate is set to 0.3.
For our graph encoder, we implement a 3-layer LightGCN. The GNN layers operate on the 128-dimensional shared entity embeddings which serve as the initial node features. For the text encoder, we use a pre-trained PubMedBERT model (Devlin et al., 2019). The 768-dimensional output from the BERT model is frozen and projected to the 128-dimensional embedding space via a linear layer.
For the Synergy-Aware module, the CrossAttentionModel is configured with 4 heads and a dropout of 0.1. For the temporal GRUs, we configure 1-layer GRUs that take a 256-dimensional concatenated (graph + text) input and output a 128-dimensional hidden state.
For the loss functions, we use Focal Loss for the recommendation task with  = 0.25 and  = 2.0. For the total contrastive loss, the main balancing weight  is 0.3. Internally, the contrastive temperature  is 0.2, and both  and  weights are set to 0.05. The temperature for dynamic synergy retrieval  is 0.1.
We conduct training for 50 epochs using the AdamW optimizer with a batch size of 16, an initial learning rate of 5e-4, and a weight decay of 1e-5.
5.4.3. Sampling Approach Given the limited availability of publicly accessible EHR
data, we utilize bootstrapping techniques in this phase, following the methodology outlined in (Yang et al., 2021b). As discussed in (Picheny et al., 2010) and (Dauji and Bhargava, 2016), this technique is particularly effective for small sample size scenarios.
5.5. Performance Comparison In this section, we compare our proposed SynGSA-
Rec model with various state-of-the-art baseline methods to evaluate its effectiveness across multiple dimensions of recommendation accuracy. For baseline methods with publicly available source code, we utilize their original implementations and apply the optimal parameter settings as reported in their respective papers. In cases where the original code is not provided, we carefully retrain the models following

the detailed configurations described in their literature. We conduct extensive evaluations on both the MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV datasets using four primary metrics: Jaccard, F1score, PR-AUC, and ROC-AUC. The comprehensive comparative results for all models are summarized in Table 3.
Analyzing the baseline models, we observe distinct performance characteristics across different technical paradigms. Traditional sequence-based models, such as the Transformer and GAMENet, leverage longitudinal patient history to predict current medication combinations. While GAMENet improves upon basic attention mechanisms by integrating medication interaction graphs and a memory bank, its heavy reliance on standardized clinical codes limits its ability to interpret unstructured bedside events. Grasp attempts to enhance health status representations by incorporating knowledge from similar patients, yet it still struggles with the semantic complexity of heterogeneous medical data. Knowledge-driven approaches like SafeDrug and MoleRec represent a significant shift by integrating molecular structures and substructure-aware representations. While these methods excel at modeling specific pharmacological properties, they remain inherently constrained by standardized coding systems. Even BiMoRec, which introduces 3D molecular modalities to resolve structural ambiguity, fails to address the critical clinical context missing from infusion records. Recent advanced models like Trans and CrossMed utilize temporal graph convolutions and cross-level interactions to capture high-resolution health trends. Although these models achieve high accuracy on benchmark metrics, they often treat clinical entities as isolated topological nodes. They overlook the intrinsic therapeutic synergy--the logical transition from rapid immediate intervention to stable oral maintenance--which is essential for capturing a patient's true treatment trajectory.
Our proposed SynGSA-Rec model consistently outperforms all state-of-the-art baselines across both datasets for every evaluation metric. This superior performance is primarily attributed to several key innovations within our framework. First, SynGSA-Rec successfully unlocks the value of previously ignored infusion records by leveraging textual descriptions, which effectively completes the clinical logic chain between immediate interventions and long-term maintenance. By explicitly modeling the therapeutic synergy between these two phases, our framework generates more coherent and patient-specific representations that reflect real-world clinical strategies. Furthermore, the two-stage contrastive learning mechanism addresses the long-standing gap between topological graph structures and intrinsic clinical semantics. This alignment ensures that the learned embeddings are not only structurally comprehensive but also therapeutically logical. By bridging the gap between unstructured bedside events and structured prescriptions through semantic alignment, SynGSA-Rec establishes a more robust representational foundation, ultimately leading to significantly more accurate and clinically relevant medication recommendations.

6. Discussions
In this section, we conduct an in-depth analysis of the experimental outcomes previously discussed. Additionally we carry out a series of supplementary experiments to confirm the comprehensiveness and rationale of our approach, to ensure its robustness and validate its advancements in the field.

6.1. Effectiveness Analysis The comparative experiments above showcase the su-
perior performance of our proposed SynGSA-Rec model. Previous medication recommendation studies typically rely on standardized clinical codes, such as ICD or ATC, to represent medical entities. However, in real-world datasets like MIMIC, a significant portion of clinical data, particularly infusion medications, lacks a standardized coding system. Consequently, these vital interventions are often excluded from existing models. Given that infusion medications often represent immediate clinical interventions while prescription medications typically provide stable maintenance, their exclusion makes it impossible for models to account for the inherent therapeutic synergy between these two distinct treatment phases. Missing this critical clinical logic ultimately limits the model's predictive accuracy and practical utility. Moreover, while textual descriptions provide rich clinical insights, current methods fail to align this semantic depth with the graphical structure. This lack of cross-modal alignment prevents models from bridging the gap between the structural patterns in the graph and their real-world clinical roles.
To address these issues, we use textual descriptions as a bridge to integrate both infusion and prescription medications into a unified space while explicitly modeling their underlying synergy. Subsequently, our two-stage contrastive learning mechanism aligns graph-based structural embeddings with their semantic counterparts. By leveraging the synergy-aware semantic view, which inherently captures the latent therapeutic logic of medications,this approach effectively recovers the clinical synergy and cross-modal correlations that are lost in traditional models.

Notably, consistent with observations in , adding clinical notes (e.g., ClinicalBERT) does not yield additional advantages for the CMR task compared to large-scale biomedical literature pretraining. The overall high performance across all PLMs validates the robustness of our synergy-guided framework (Lv et al., 2025).

6.8. Limitations Despite the promising performance of SynGSA-Rec,
several limitations should be acknowledged. First, while our synergy-aware mechanism successfully captures therapeutic patterns through textual semantics, the quantification of synergy strength via cosine similarity may oversimplify complex clinical relationships. Other factors such as temporal dynamics between intervention and maintenance phases, or patient-specific pharmacokinetic variations, are

not explicitly modeled in the current similarity measure. Second, our framework depends on sufficient historical visit records to retrieve relevant therapeutic precedents. For patients with limited medical history or first-time admissions, the dynamic synergy fusion may not function optimally, which is a common challenge in longitudinal recommendation systems (Tan et al., 2025). Additionally, our two-stage contrastive learning mechanism introduces several hyperparameters (e.g., , , , ) that require careful tuning, and the optimal settings may vary across different datasets or clinical contexts.
7. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose SynGSA-Rec, a framework that explicitly models the Therapeutic Synergy between infusion and prescription medications. By leveraging textual descriptions, we incorporate vital clinical logic previously overlooked in ICU records. Our two-stage contrastive learning mechanism aligns graph embeddings with semantic views to ensure medication representations are both structurally comprehensive and therapeutically coherent. Experimental results on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV demonstrate that SynGSA-Rec significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines. These findings validate our synergy-guided approach for enhancing the clinical logicality of medication recommendation systems.
